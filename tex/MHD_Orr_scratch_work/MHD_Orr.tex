\section{Deriving Orr in MHD}
In the following, I'm repeating my Orr notes from Waleffe's class line-by-line except with a magnetic field included.

Start with our linearized equations, but take the background flow to be uniform shear, i.e., $U = Sz$, so
\begin{equation} \label{eq:lin-momentum}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \mathbf{u} + wS \mathbf{e}_x + Sz \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathbf{u} = - \nabla p + \frac{1}{M_A^2} \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathbf{b} + \frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}} \nabla^2 \mathbf{u},
\end{equation}

\begin{equation} \label{eq:lin-induction}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \mathbf{b} + Sz \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathbf{b} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathbf{u} + b_z S \mathbf{e}_x + \frac{1}{\mathrm{Rm}} \nabla^2 \mathbf{b},
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0, \qquad \nabla \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0.
\end{equation}

Next, assume solutions of the form $f(x, y, z, t) = \hat{f}(t) \exp{[i \mathbf{k}(t) \cdot \mathbf{r}]} + c.c.$, insert that, and then out of laziness drop the hats and the $+ c.c.$ to get
\begin{equation} \label{eq:FT-momentum}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \mathbf{u} + i(\dot{\mathbf{k}} \cdot \mathbf{r}) \mathbf{u} + wS \mathbf{e}_x + Sz i k_x \mathbf{u} = - i \mathbf{k} p + \frac{1}{M_A^2} i k_x \mathbf{b} - \frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}} |\mathbf{k}|^2 \mathbf{u},
\end{equation}

\begin{equation} \label{eq:FT-induction}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \mathbf{b} + i(\dot{\mathbf{k}} \cdot \mathbf{r}) \mathbf{b} + Sz i k_x \mathbf{b} = i k_x \mathbf{u} + b_z S \mathbf{e}_x - \frac{1}{\mathrm{Rm}} |\mathbf{k}|^2 \mathbf{b},
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0, \qquad \mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0.
\end{equation}

From the first equation (or the second), inspecting the only terms that have explicit $x$-dependence reveals
\begin{equation} \label{eq:kxdot}
    i \dot{k}_x = 0,
\end{equation}
and likewise inspecting the $y$-dependence reveals 
\begin{equation} \label{eq:kydot}
    i \dot{k}_y = 0.
\end{equation}
So $k_x$ and $k_y$ are constant. Inspecting the terms that have explicit $z$-dependence in either equation reveals
\begin{equation} \label{eq:kzdot}
    \dot{k}_z + S k_x = 0
\end{equation}
So
\begin{equation}
    k_z(t) = k_z(0) - S k_x t
\end{equation}
just like in hydro!

Next, we want to eliminate the pressure. Dot the momentum equation with $\mathbf{k}$ to get
\begin{equation} \label{eq:momentum-dot-k}
\mathbf{k} \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \mathbf{u} + wS k_x = - i |\mathbf{k}|^2 p,
\end{equation}
where the $\dot{\mathbf{k}}$ terms went away by making use of equations \eqref{eq:kxdot}-\eqref{eq:kzdot}, and the other terms that vanished did so because of $\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0$. Now use $\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$ to write 
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{k} \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \dot{\mathbf{k}} = 0.
\end{equation}
Insert our expression for $\dot{\mathbf{k}}$ and rearrange to find $\mathbf{k} \cdot \partial_t \mathbf{u}$, then insert that into Eq.~\eqref{eq:momentum-dot-k} and rearrange to solve for the pressure to get
\begin{equation} \label{eq:pressure}
    i p = \frac{-2wSk_z}{|\mathbf{k}|^2}.
\end{equation}

Insert this back into Eq.~\eqref{eq:FT-momentum} to get
\begin{equation} \label{eq:momentum-no-p}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \mathbf{u} + wS \mathbf{e}_x = \mathbf{k} \frac{2wSk_z}{|\mathbf{k}|^2} + \frac{1}{M_A^2} i k_x \mathbf{b} - \frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}} |\mathbf{k}|^2 \mathbf{u}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{A minor diversion into the energy equations for some reason}
Dot this with $\mathbf{u}$ to get the KE evolution equation
\begin{equation} \label{eq:KE-evolution}
\frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial}{\partial t} |\mathbf{u}|^2 + wuS = \frac{1}{M_A^2} i k_x \mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{u} - \frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}} |\mathbf{k}|^2 |\mathbf{u}|^2.
\end{equation}
Similarly, the ME evolution equation is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:ME-evolution}
\frac{1}{2 M_A^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t} |\mathbf{b}|^2 = \frac{1}{M_A^2} i k_x \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{b} + \frac{1}{M_A^2}b_x b_z S - \frac{1}{\mathrm{Rm} M_A^2} |\mathbf{k}|^2 |\mathbf{b}|^2.
\end{equation}
Something is wrong. One of the $\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{b}$ terms should have a minus sign so that they cancel out when adding these two equations together. Hmm.

For now don't worry about it. Add the two equations together, assume they properly cancel, and we should end up with
\begin{equation} \label{eq:TE-evolution}
\frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial}{\partial t} ( |\mathbf{u}|^2 + \frac{1}{M_A^2} |\mathbf{b}|^2 ) = - wuS + b_xb_z S \frac{1}{M_A^2} - \frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}} |\mathbf{k}|^2 |\mathbf{u}|^2 - \frac{1}{\mathrm{Rm} M_A^2} |\mathbf{k}|^2 |\mathbf{b}|^2.
\end{equation}

At this stage in my notes for the hydro case I have ``Notice that production/dissipation is maximized for $v=0$, $u=-w$." We similarly want $b_y = 0$ and $b_x = b_z$.

\textbf{In hindsight the issue is that I needed to dot the equations with $\mathbf{u}^*$ and $\mathbf{b}^*$, not $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.}

\subsection{Done with that minor diversion}
Isolating the $z$ components of our u and b equations gives
\begin{equation} \label{eq:w-no-p}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t} w = \frac{2wSk_z^2}{|\mathbf{k}|^2} + \frac{1}{M_A^2} i k_x b_z - \frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}} |\mathbf{k}|^2 w
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation} \label{eq:bz}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t} b_z = i k_x w - \frac{1}{\mathrm{Rm}} |\mathbf{k}|^2 b_z.
\end{equation}
At this stage in the hydro notes, the $w$ equation can be directly solved by inserting the solution we already have for $\mathbf{k}$ and noting we have a linear, first-order ODE with non-constant coefficients, which can generally be solved by using integration factors.

For our case, I wonder if the thing to do is to transform to Els\"{a}sser variables (often written as $\mathbf{z}^{\pm} \equiv \mathbf{u} \pm M_A^{-2} \mathbf{b}$ -- you can see them mentioned briefly in the Wikipedia article for MHD turbulence) and hope that the same thing emerges. Take the first equation, and add the second multiplied by $\pm 1/M_A^2$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \left( w \pm \frac{b_z}{M_A^2} \right) = \frac{2wSk_z^2}{|\mathbf{k}|^2} + \frac{i k_x}{M_A^2} (b_z \pm w) - |\mathbf{k}|^2 \left( \frac{w}{\mathrm{Re}} \pm \frac{b_z}{\mathrm{Rm}M_A^2} \right).
\end{equation}
Now introduce the notation $\mathbf{z}^{\pm} \equiv \mathbf{u} \pm M_A^{-2} \mathbf{b}$, then this can be written as
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial}{\partial t} z_z^{\pm} = \frac{Sk_z^2}{|\mathbf{k}|^2}(z_z^+ + z_z^-) + \frac{i k_x}{M_A^2} (b_z \pm w) - |\mathbf{k}|^2 \left( \frac{w}{\mathrm{Re}} \pm \frac{b_z}{\mathrm{Rm}M_A^2} \right).
\end{equation}
This is usually easier when you work in units where $M_A \to 1$...

In any case, this isn't going anywhere. I was hoping I could use this transformation to decouple the $w$ and $b_z$ equations (at least for $\mathrm{Pm} = 1$) into one equation for $z_z^+$ and a separate, decoupled equation for $z_z^-$, but already from that term proportional to $S$ we can see that these equations will not decouple.
